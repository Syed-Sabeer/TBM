Our position

A tote bag is not automatically a good deed

A cotton tote has to be used a lot of times before it beats the plastic bag it replaced. Everyone in this industry knows that, and most of us quietly avoid saying it. We would rather be the supplier that says it out loud, because it changes what a good bag looks like.

A bag that gets used two hundred times is worth more, environmentally, than a bag with a leaf printed on it that gets thrown away after one conference. So the most useful thing we can do is make bags people keep: correct fabric weight for the job, seams that survive the washing machine, handles bar-tacked where they take the load. Durability is the sustainability feature that actually does the work.

“The greenest bag we sell is the one your customer is still carrying in four years.”

Lia Moreau, Sustainability & Compliance

On top of that we run proper certified programmes, because a claim without a certificate behind it is just marketing — and increasingly your customers' procurement teams know it.

Supply chain

Fourteen mills, all of them visited

We do not buy through agents. Every mill in our chain has been walked by our own head of sourcing, and the two largest carry a current SMETA 4-pillar audit.

What we audit for

  • Wages at or above the legal minimum, paid on time, with records
  • No under-age labour, verified against documents not claims
  • Working hours within local law, overtime voluntary and paid
  • Fire exits, machine guarding and chemical storage
  • Effluent treatment at every wet-processing site

What happens when something fails

A finding does not end the relationship on its own — walking away usually just moves the problem somewhere less visible. We agree a corrective action plan with a date, re-audit against it, and stop buying if the date passes without movement. We have ended two mill relationships that way since 2019.

Serious findings — under-age labour, forced labour, withheld documents — are different. Those end the relationship immediately and we say so in the contract.

Straight answers

What we do not claim

We are not carbon neutral. We have not bought offsets to say we are. We measure scope 1 and 2 emissions at the three warehouses and we are working on scope 3, which is where almost all of it actually sits. When we have a number we trust, it will go on this page.

"Eco" is not a certification. The eco badge on our listings means the item is in a GOTS or GRS programme, or is made from recycled content, and each listing says which. It is not a general environmental score and we will not let it be read as one.

Biodegradable is not a claim we make. Cotton in a landfill does not helpfully disappear, and "biodegradable" polyester additives have not convinced us. We would rather sell you a bag that lasts.

We are not the whole supply chain. Our certificates cover the mills and the conversion. They do not cover the cotton farm four steps upstream, and any supplier telling you otherwise is overreaching.

Ask us for evidence. If we cannot produce a document for a claim, treat the claim as marketing — including ours.
