The beginning

One container and a spreadsheet

In 2013 we landed a single container of cotton totes in Long Beach, rented eight hundred square feet of a shared warehouse in Vernon, and sold the lot in fourteen weeks to print shops who were tired of being told "call for pricing."

The second container went the same way. So did the third. What we had was not a clever product — it was the same cotton tote everyone else sold — it was a number you could rely on. A buyer could quote their customer on Monday knowing the price would still be the price on Friday.

“Every supplier we bought from had two prices: the one on the sheet, and the one you got if you pushed. We decided to only have the second one.”

Maya Castellanos, co-founder

That is still the whole idea. There is no public price list on this site, because a public list is a fiction that everyone then negotiates away from. Instead every approved account carries one rate card, agreed up front, applied to every person who logs in from that company — whether it is the owner placing a container order or a junior buyer topping up 250 pieces on a Tuesday afternoon.

Thirteen years later we hold stock on both coasts and in Texas, run our own decoration floor in Los Angeles, and still answer the phone on the second ring.

      How we work

      Three things we will not trade away

      One price per account

      Your rate card is agreed once and applied consistently. We do not run a "list price" that nobody pays, and we do not quietly reprice a repeat order because volume dipped one quarter.

      Stock we actually hold

      The quantity on an item page is the quantity on the rack, fed from the mill every morning. If it says 18,400 pieces, you can commit 18,400 pieces to your customer.

      A person who knows you

      Every account has a named rep, not a queue. They know your programmes, your in-hands dates and the two colours you always run, and they pick up on the second ring.

      And one thing we are not

      We are not the cheapest supplier in the category, and on a straight non-woven giveaway we will occasionally lose to an importer working on two points. What we are is predictable: the stock is where we said it would be, the price is the price we agreed, and the item number on the invoice is the one your customer recognises. If the only thing that matters on a particular job is landed cost, tell us — sometimes the honest answer is that we are not the right supplier for that one.
